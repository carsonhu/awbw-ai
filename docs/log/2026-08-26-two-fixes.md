# A dead attacker that would not disappear, and JakeMan's missing precedence

> Both found by watching a replay rather than reading a number. `combatInfo`
> was carrying the whole unit row where AWBW carries five fields, and the port
> let a walk outbid an attack. JakeMan is stronger for it: 67.2% -> 58.4%.

## The unit that died and stayed

A tank on 2 HP attacked one on 8, lost the counterattack, and its sprite stayed
on the board at 0 HP for the rest of the game — only ever the *attacker*, and
in no other replay.

Everything checked first came back clean, which is where the time went. A
killed defender is written exactly as AWBW writes one, at 0 HP on its tile and
not as a null; our snapshots hold no 0 HP units and neither do AWBW's; all 80
destroyed units in one replay are absent from every later snapshot, and a real
AWBW game scores the same. The difference was the size of the record:

| | real AWBW | ours |
|---|---|---|
| `combatInfo` attacker and defender | **5 fields** — id, x, y, ammo, HP | **27** — the whole unit row |

AWBW puts a *delta* in `combatInfo` and the full row only in `Move` and
`Build`. A full row reads as an assertion that the unit exists, so the mover is
put back on its tile at zero HP and stays; the defender survives this because
the viewer removes it on the HP. Now trimmed to five fields.

The round trip is unaffected — the same four games verify at 99.837% either
way, identical divergence counts. Those 29 move-fuel and 4 unit-position
divergences are *pre-existing* and unexplained (a snapshot reports 99 fuel
where the engine has spent to 96): the next thing to look at here.

## JakeMan walked past fights it should have taken

DefendPeace does not weigh attacking against travelling. `GetFreeDudes` returns
`findBestAttack`'s pick outright, and `Travel` is a later module commented "if
no attack/capture actions are available now, just move around". The port
flattened that queue into one score, and `APPROACH` at 100 a tile put a walk in
the same band as an attack worth a few hundred: **24 of 412 moves** on the
training map left a positive attack unused.

The scoring itself was a faithful port — `damage - loss`, `STAY_ALIVE_BIAS`,
the first-strike weight, the divide for an already-hurt defender all match
`findBestAttack` line for line. Only the precedence was missing.
`ATTACK_FLOOR` restores it and declined attacks go to **0 of 371**; a losing
exchange keeps its negative score and still ranks below walking away, which is
what a null from `findBestAttack` does.

**It made JakeMan meaningfully stronger**, which is the point: `ppo-jake2`
rated 67.2% ±2.3 against it before and **58.4% ±2.5** after. Every rating
against JakeMan taken earlier is against a weaker bot and does not transfer.
The ladder is otherwise unchanged — 97% against `greedy` over 30 games — and
the mirror stays balanced at 24-16, against 22-18 before.

**A caution.** Four readings here were wrong before they were right: a board
called unbalanced on one game's property count, a 5,980-point scoring gap that
was two threat maps built at different points in the turn, a ghost count that
included the snapshots from before each unit died, and a recon spam a real map
does not show. Each looked like a finding.
